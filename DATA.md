# Data

The data is held outside this repository and **none of it is ever committed** —
these are identifiable clinical transcripts. That includes notebook outputs,
which carry tokenised transcripts and are the easiest way to leak them without
noticing. Point `SLDA_DATA_DIR` at the folder holding the CSVs below.

## The prep is correct

Verified against the zip: the median ratio of combined-to-source words is exactly
1.000 across all 329 participants and none exceeds 1.5×. `filename.split('@')[0]`
is a valid participant key.

## The corpus is extremely unbalanced, and that is real

Participant `u00001870` genuinely recorded 386 diaries — the zip confirms 386,
the CSV has 385 after cleaning. It is not a duplication bug.

- 1 participant = 26% of all words (29% of tokens after preprocessing)
- top 10 = 48%
- bottom half of participants = 4.9%
- 77 participants have a single entry
- median 4 entries per participant, max 386

This is the single most consequential property of the dataset. Left uncorrected
it makes every topic model return that one person's vocabulary. See
`RESULTS.md` section 3.

## Text quality

**Repaired.** 942 corrupted punctuation marks across 74 documents. UTF-8
punctuation lost its high bits somewhere in the chain, so U+2019 (E2 80 99)
survives as bytes 62 00 19 — `b` plus a control character. `it's` became
`itb\x19s`, which after punctuation-stripping tokenised as `itbs`. That is why
the project's hand-written stopword list contained "itbs", "donbt", "iaao": they
were symptoms of the corruption being treated as noise words.

**Repaired.** 390 of the 2,184 R training transcripts carry a curly apostrophe
(U+2019). tidytext's `stop_words` contains `i'm` with a *straight* quote, so the
curly form survived at 1,022 occurrences, sixth commonest token in the corpus. A
further 139 rows carry doubly cp1252-mangled UTF-8 and need two round trips.

**Dropped, not repaired.** An older corruption replaced the apostrophe with a
literal ASCII `u`: `it's` → `itus`, `I'm` → `ium`, `don't` → `donut`,
`they're` → `theyure`. These are indistinguishable from ordinary words at the
byte level and "donut" is a real word, so repair would sometimes invent one.
The family is 28 forms wider than the three that were originally noticed, and is
now generated by rule in both `build_corpus.py` and `slda_stopwords.R`.
